A small request — please read everything on this page, slowly. It was written for you.

From a Class of 10th, still yours,

It's strange how a school gate you walked through every morning for years can still show up in your dreams. The chalk-green boards, the roll number you knew by heart, the bell that decided when we ran and when we sat still — none of it left, really. It just moved somewhere quieter, inside.

I passed out of 10th standard from this school, and every Teachers' Day since, I find myself thinking less about the syllabus and more about the people who taught it. The ones who stayed back to explain one more sum. The ones who scolded us because they expected better, not because they enjoyed it. The ones who remembered our names even in a school full of them.

This little page is for them — and for every student, past or present, who owes a small, permanent part of who they are to a teacher at Chintamaniswar.

— Jagdish Govt. High School, Chintamaniswar · Batch of 10th

Why today?

September 5th belongs to a teacher who became a President

India celebrates Teachers' Day on the birthday of Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan — a philosopher and teacher who went on to become the country's President. When his students once wanted to celebrate his birthday, he asked them to observe the day in honour of all teachers instead. That request is why classrooms across the country, including ours, pause today to say thank you.

गुरुर्ब्रह्मा गुरुर्विष्णुः गुरुर्देवो महेश्वरः।
गुरुः साक्षात् परब्रह्म तस्मै श्री गुरवे नमः॥ An ancient verse placing the teacher alongside the divine — a reminder of how deeply this idea runs in our culture.
